Frequently Asked Questions

Which BMR formula is most accurate?
The Mifflin-St Jeor equation (2005) is generally considered the most accurate for most people, with a margin of error of ±10%. The Katch-McArdle formula is more accurate if you know your exact body fat percentage, as it uses lean body mass. Harris-Benedict (revised 1984) is still widely used. Our calculator shows all three so you can compare.
How much water should I drink per day?
General guideline is 35ml per kg of body weight. A 70kg person needs about 2.45 liters. However, this increases with physical activity (add 500ml per hour of exercise), hot/humid climate (add 300-500ml), and for breastfeeding women (add 700ml). Our calculator adjusts all these factors automatically.
What is TDEE and how is it different from BMR?
BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the calories your body needs at complete rest — just to keep organs functioning. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is BMR multiplied by your activity factor. If you're sedentary, TDEE = BMR × 1.2. Moderately active: BMR × 1.55. Very active: BMR × 1.9. TDEE is what you actually burn per day.
Is BMI a reliable health indicator?
BMI is a useful screening tool but has limitations. It doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat, so athletes may have a high BMI despite being very healthy. It also doesn't account for where fat is distributed. For a complete picture, consider waist circumference, body fat percentage, and other metabolic markers alongside BMI.
