YouTube SEO FAQ

What is the ideal YouTube title length?
YouTube displays up to 60 characters in search results. Keep your title between 50-70 characters. Place the most important keyword in the first 40 characters. Our tool automatically shows the character count and flags titles that are too long or short.
How many tags should I use on YouTube?
YouTube allows up to 500 characters worth of tags. Use 15-30 tags — a mix of specific, medium, and broad tags. Start with your exact topic, then add variations, related terms, and broad category terms. Our tool generates all three types for you.
Does YouTube description help with SEO?
Yes, absolutely! YouTube's algorithm reads your description to understand what your video is about. Include your main keyword in the first 125 characters (visible before "Show more"), add timestamps for better engagement, and include related keywords naturally throughout. A well-written description can significantly boost your rankings.

YouTube SEO Tips Beyond Metadata

  • Put your main keyword in the video filename before uploading. YouTube's crawler reads the filename as a relevance signal. "chicken-biryani-recipe-beginners.mp4" is better than "video_final_v3.mp4".
  • Add chapters/timestamps to your description. Videos with timestamp chapters appear in YouTube search results with chapter previews, dramatically increasing CTR. Format: "0:00 Introduction | 2:30 Soaking the rice | 5:15 Preparing the masala".
  • Create a custom thumbnail with a face and text. Videos with a human face in the thumbnail consistently outperform thumbnails without one in CTR studies. The face should convey an emotion relevant to the video — excitement, surprise, or clear instruction-giving.
  • Respond to every comment in the first 48 hours. Early comment engagement signals to YouTube's algorithm that your video generates discussion, which increases its distribution to non-subscribers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tags should a YouTube video have?

YouTube allows up to 500 characters of tags. Research by SEO analysts consistently shows that 8–15 specific, relevant tags outperform either too few tags (missing semantic context) or too many generic tags (diluting relevance signals). The most important tags are: (1) your exact target keyword phrase, (2) the most common search variations of that phrase, (3) your channel's topic categories (so YouTube correctly categorises your channel), and (4) 3–5 related but distinct topics the video covers. Tags are far less important than title and description for ranking — Google has stated that tags are a minor factor — but they still help YouTube understand the video's topic for its recommendation algorithm.

Does keyword stuffing in descriptions hurt rankings?

Yes. YouTube's algorithm is sophisticated enough to identify keyword stuffing (unnatural repetition of the same phrase) and it can hurt your video's ranking and viewer trust. The description should read naturally — as if you wrote it for a human audience, not an algorithm. Mention your target keyword 2–3 times maximum in the first 150 words. The remainder of the description should add genuine value: explain what the video covers, include timestamps, link to related content on your channel, and add a call to action (subscribe, comment). A description written for viewers that naturally includes relevant keywords consistently outperforms stuffed descriptions in A/B tests.

How long does it take to rank a new YouTube video in search?

New channels with no history take longer to rank than established channels. A new channel video may take 2–4 weeks to appear in search results for competitive keywords, and may never rank on page 1 for high-volume terms against established channels with millions of subscribers. The most effective strategy for new channels is to target long-tail, low-competition keywords — specific, detailed search queries with lower monthly search volume but far less competition. "Biryani recipe" is brutally competitive; "biryani recipe for beginners without pressure cooker" is achievable for a new channel. The YouTube SEO Optimizer is specifically designed to identify these long-tail opportunities.

Should I optimise for YouTube Search or YouTube Suggested?

Both matter, but they require different strategies. YouTube Search optimisation (via title, description, tags) gets you discovered by people actively searching. YouTube Suggested optimisation (getting recommended after related videos) is driven by watch time, retention rate, and click-through rate on impressions. For new channels, YouTube Search is typically more accessible because you can rank for specific queries even without an audience. As your channel grows and your videos demonstrate high retention and engagement, the Suggested algorithm begins distributing your content broadly — this is where exponential growth comes from. Focus on Search SEO first to build your initial audience; watch time optimisation (pacing, editing, hooks) will then activate the Suggested algorithm.

Is it worth creating Shorts for SEO alongside regular videos?

YouTube Shorts (vertical videos under 60 seconds) have a separate recommendation system from long-form content and do not directly improve your long-form video rankings. However, Shorts can rapidly grow your subscriber base, which indirectly helps long-form video performance by increasing the "initial boost" your new uploads receive from subscribers. A common strategy: create a Short as a teaser clip from each long-form video, with a call to action to watch the full video. This drives viewers from the high-impression Shorts feed to your monetisable long-form content. For Pakistani creators, Shorts in Urdu/Hindi can reach an enormous regional audience through YouTube's aggressive distribution of short-form content.

What is the best upload frequency for growing a YouTube channel in Pakistan?

Consistency matters more than frequency. Publishing one well-optimised video per week and maintaining that schedule for 6–12 months outperforms publishing five videos in one week then going silent. YouTube's algorithm favours channels that signal predictable content production — it learns your upload pattern and begins anticipating your uploads for distribution. For Pakistani creators balancing day jobs or studies, once-weekly uploads are achievable and sufficient. Focus on quality (good lighting, clear audio, a proper introduction that hooks viewers in the first 30 seconds) over volume. The first 30 seconds of your video determine whether viewers stay or leave — this is the single most impactful production improvement most creators can make.
