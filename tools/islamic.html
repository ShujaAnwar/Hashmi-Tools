Our Approach to Islamic Calculations

Islamic jurisprudence contains multiple valid scholarly positions on many calculation questions. Where differences exist, we follow these principles:

  • Prayer times — we support multiple calculation methods (ISNA, MWL, Karachi/University of Islamic Sciences) so users can select the method used by their local mosque or Islamic authority
  • Zakat Nisab — we offer both the gold Nisab (85g gold) and silver Nisab (595g silver) thresholds, noting that most contemporary scholars recommend the silver Nisab as it includes more people and is closer to the Prophet's ﷺ practice
  • Inheritance — Hanafi jurisprudence is used as the default, being the prevalent madhab in Pakistan; the tool notes that minor differences exist in other schools
  • Hijri calendar — we use the calculated astronomical calendar while noting that official dates in Pakistan are confirmed by the Ruet-e-Hilal Committee through moon sighting

For all faith-critical decisions, we encourage users to verify with a qualified Islamic scholar (Mufti or Imam) in their community. These tools are reference aids, not fatwas.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which prayer time calculation method should I use?

For Pakistani users, the University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi (UISK) method is the most widely used and is the basis for prayer timetables published by most mosques in Pakistan. If you're abroad, use the method followed by your local mosque — ISNA for North America, MWL for Europe and most of the Muslim world, UISK for South Asia.

Is the Zakat calculator accurate for all types of wealth?

The calculator handles the most common categories: cash, bank savings, gold, silver, business goods, and investment accounts. More complex Zakat questions — on pension funds, shares in companies, agricultural produce, livestock, or real estate held for investment — involve scholarly differences and should be verified with a qualified Zakat scholar or Islamic finance institution.

Why might the prayer times differ slightly from my mosque's timetable?

Prayer time calculations depend on the calculation method (affecting Fajr and Isha angles), the Asr method (Hanafi uses 2× shadow length, Shafi uses 1×), and rounding convention (some timetables add 2–5 minutes as a safety margin to Iqamat times). Even the same method can produce differences of 1–3 minutes based on precise coordinates. Always use your local mosque's timetable as the definitive reference for congregation times.

Does the Hijri date shown match the Saudi Umm al-Qura calendar?

Not always. The Umm al-Qura calendar is a calculated calendar used for official Saudi dates. Our converter uses the Kuwaiti algorithm, which produces results very close to Umm al-Qura but may differ by 1 day for some dates. For official Saudi dates (for Hajj registration, official documents), verify with the official Umm al-Qura calendar at saudiembassy.net or islamicfinder.org.

How Prayer Times Are Calculated: Accurate prayer times depend on your geographical location (latitude and longitude) and the calculation method used by your local religious authority. The Karachi method (18° Fajr, 18° Isha) is standard for Pakistani and South Asian users; the Muslim World League method (18° Fajr, 17° Isha) is used in Europe and North America; the Egyptian General Authority method (19.5° Fajr, 17.5° Isha) is used in Egypt and the Gulf. Frequently Asked Questions

Which prayer time calculation method should I use in Pakistan?

For Pakistan, the University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi method is the standard reference used by the majority of mosques and religious institutions. This method uses 18° for both Fajr and Isha angles, which produces prayer times consistent with traditional Pakistani religious practice. The Pakistan government's Ministry of Religious Affairs also publishes official prayer time tables using this method. If you find discrepancies between our calculated times and your local mosque's announced times, ensure you are using the Karachi method and have entered your precise city coordinates — Pakistan spans multiple time zones and latitude ranges, and even within one city, minor coordinate differences can shift prayer times by a few minutes.

Is the Hijri calendar conversion accurate?

The Hijri calendar is a lunar calendar of 354–355 days per year, approximately 11 days shorter than the Gregorian solar calendar. Conversion between the two calendars is algorithmic — the standard Tabular Islamic Calendar (used for historical and administrative purposes) is calculated mathematically and is precisely accurate. However, the actual beginning of each Hijri month (and therefore exact dates for Ramadan, Eid, etc.) in traditional Islamic practice depends on physical sighting of the new moon (hilal), which varies by location and religious authority. The Converter uses the standard astronomical calculation, which aligns with most countries' officially announced dates but may differ by 1 day from moon-sighting-based rulings in some regions or communities.

How do I calculate Zakat on investments, gold, and business inventory?

Zakat is due on specific categories of zakatable assets: gold and silver (at their current market value); cash and bank balances; trade goods and business inventory (at their current market value, not cost price); receivables (money owed to you that you expect to recover); and shares in companies (typically calculated on the per-share market value, with some scholars calculating only on the zakatable assets of the underlying company). Assets excluded from Zakat include: personal residence, personal vehicle, household goods, and business equipment used in production (not for sale). The Zakat Calculator guides you through each category with clear prompts, then applies the 2.5% rate to your net zakatable assets above the nisab threshold.
