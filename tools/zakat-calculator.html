    About Zakat — Islamic Guidelines

    Nisab: Zakat is obligatory when your net wealth equals or exceeds the Nisab threshold maintained for one full lunar year (Hawl). The silver nisab (612.36g) is more commonly used by scholars today as it benefits the poor.


    Rate: Zakat is exactly 2.5% (1/40th) of your net zakatable wealth.


    What is Zakatable: Cash, bank balances, gold/silver, business inventory, investments, property bought for resale, and debts owed to you.


    What is NOT Zakatable: Personal home, personal car, personal jewelry used regularly (Hanafi: jewelry worn by women is exempt), tools of trade, household items.


    Disclaimer: This calculator is for guidance only. Please consult a qualified Islamic scholar (Mufti) for complex situations.

    ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

    What is Zakat? ▼

    Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam — an obligatory annual charity of 2.5% on wealth above the Nisab threshold, given to eligible recipients.

    What is Nisab? ▼

    Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must possess before Zakat becomes obligatory. It equals the value of 87.48g of gold or 612.36g of silver — whichever applies.

    What assets are Zakatable? ▼

    Cash, gold, silver, stocks/investments, business inventory, and receivable debts are all subject to Zakat. Personal use items (home, car, clothes) are generally exempt.

    Is 2.5% the correct Zakat rate? ▼

    2.5% (1/40th) is the standard Zakat rate on most forms of wealth. Agricultural produce has different rates (5% or 10%). Consult a scholar for complex situations.

    Do I pay Zakat on my salary? ▼

    Zakat is not on income directly — it's on savings. If your savings (after deducting debts) have been above Nisab for a full lunar year (Hawl), 2.5% Zakat is due on them.

    Zakat Calculator — How to Calculate Zakat on Your Wealth

    Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam — an obligatory annual payment of 2.5% of a Muslim's qualifying accumulated wealth above the Nisab threshold. Unlike income tax, Zakat is not calculated on income earned; it is calculated on net wealth that has been held for a full lunar year (Hawl) and exceeds the Nisab minimum. Understanding exactly what qualifies as Zakatable wealth, what the Nisab threshold is in Pakistani Rupees, and how to handle different asset types is the core challenge this calculator addresses.

    Paying Zakat accurately is a religious obligation with significant spiritual consequences — underpaying is a failure to fulfil the third pillar, while overpaying is not required. This calculator helps you determine your obligation precisely.

    What Is Nisab?

    Nisab is the minimum threshold of wealth above which Zakat becomes obligatory. It was defined by the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in terms of silver and gold:

    Standard Quantity Approx. PKR Value (varies with gold/silver prices) Scholarly Recommendation
    Gold Nisab87.48 grams (7.5 tola)~Rs 900,000–1,100,000Higher threshold
    Silver Nisab612.36 grams (52.5 tola)~Rs 80,000–100,000Lower threshold — most scholars recommend this as it includes more people

    Most contemporary Islamic scholars (including Darul Uloom Karachi and many South Asian Muftis) recommend using the silver Nisab because it is more consistent with the Prophet's ﷺ intent of ensuring Zakat reaches those who need it most. The calculator offers both options.

    The Zakat Calculation

    Total Zakatable Wealth = Cash + Gold (market value) + Silver (market value) + Business Assets + Investments

    Less: Short-term debts due within one year

    Net Zakatable Wealth = Total − Debts

    If Net Zakatable Wealth ≥ Nisab:
    Zakat Due = Net Zakatable Wealth × 2.5%

    Frequently Asked Questions

    When should I calculate Zakat — at the start or end of Ramadan?

    Zakat is due on the completion of one Hawl (lunar year) from when your wealth first reached Nisab. Many Muslims conveniently calculate and pay Zakat during Ramadan for the multiplied spiritual reward of the blessed month — but technically, your personal Zakat date is the anniversary of when you first held Nisab-qualifying wealth. If you're unsure of your Hawl date, using 1 Ramadan as a consistent annual date is an accepted practice.

    Is Zakat deducted automatically from Pakistani bank accounts?

    Under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980, Pakistani banks deduct Zakat at 2.5% from savings accounts that exceed the Nisab threshold on the first day of Ramadan. This is the government's Zakat collection system, separate from your personal Zakat obligation. If you wish to calculate and distribute Zakat yourself (to specific recipients), you can file a CZ-50 form (Declaration for Exemption from Zakat Deduction) at your bank to opt out of automatic deduction.

    Who are the eligible recipients (Masarif) of Zakat?

    The Quran (9:60) specifies 8 categories: the poor (Fuqara), the needy (Masakeen), Zakat administrators, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, freeing slaves (no longer applicable), those in debt (Gharimeen), in the way of Allah (Fi Sabilillah — Islamic education, da'wah), and travellers in need (Ibn as-Sabeel). The most common recipients are the poor and needy. Zakat cannot be given to one's parents, grandparents, children, grandchildren, or spouse.

    Is Zakat obligatory on gold jewellery worn by women?

    According to the Hanafi school (dominant in Pakistan), Zakat is obligatory on all gold and silver jewellery, including jewellery worn regularly by women. This is the mainstream South Asian scholarly position. Maliki, Shafi, and Hanbali schools have differing positions on worn jewellery. If you follow Hanafi jurisprudence (as most Pakistani Muslims do), include the market value of all gold jewellery in your Zakat calculation.

    Can I pay Zakat in kind (food, goods) instead of cash?

    Zakat on cash and monetary assets should generally be paid in cash or its monetary equivalent so recipients can use it for their most pressing needs. Paying Zakat in kind (food, clothing) is permissible but less flexible for recipients. Sadaqah (voluntary charity) can freely be given in kind. For Zakat al-Fitr (the separate obligatory charity of Eid ul-Fitr), the Hanafi position allows cash equivalent to the value of one sa' of food.
