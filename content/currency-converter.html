Currency Converter — Exchange Rates, Mid-Market Rate, and What You're Actually Paying

Currency conversion is one of the most practically important — and most commonly misunderstood — financial calculations people perform. Whether you're sending money to family abroad, buying products from an international store, planning a trip, or importing goods for a business, understanding the difference between the real exchange rate and what you're actually charged is essential to avoiding unnecessary costs.

Our Currency Converter pulls the mid-market exchange rate — the midpoint between the buy and sell prices on the global currency market — to give you an accurate reference rate. This is the rate you see on Google or Reuters. It is not necessarily the rate your bank, remittance service, or money changer will give you, but it's the benchmark against which you should evaluate any offer.

How Foreign Exchange Markets Work

The foreign exchange (forex) market is the world's largest financial market — over $7.5 trillion USD in daily trading volume as of 2022. Unlike stock exchanges, it has no central location; it operates as an over-the-counter (OTC) market through a global network of banks, brokers, and electronic platforms 24 hours a day, 5 days a week.

Exchange rates are determined by supply and demand. When Pakistan's exports earn more foreign exchange than the country spends on imports, demand for PKR rises and the currency strengthens. When trade deficits grow, foreign debt payments rise, or foreign investor confidence falls, PKR weakens. Central banks (like the State Bank of Pakistan) can intervene by buying or selling reserves to stabilise extreme movements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my bank give a different rate than this calculator shows?

This calculator shows the mid-market rate — the unbiased midpoint of global forex trading. Banks add a markup (spread) on top of the mid-market rate as their profit on the transaction. The difference between what you see here and what your bank charges is effectively a hidden fee. Compare this rate to your bank's rate before any transaction.

Are the rates updated in real time?

Rates are fetched from exchange rate APIs and refreshed every 30–60 minutes. For the absolute latest rate (e.g., for a large transaction timed to the second), use a dedicated forex terminal. For everyday conversions, the 30-minute-old rate is accurate enough — major currency pairs rarely move more than 0.1–0.3% in 30 minutes under normal conditions.

What is the interbank rate and can I access it?

The interbank rate is the rate at which major banks trade currency with each other — essentially the wholesale rate. Retail customers (individuals and small businesses) cannot access interbank rates directly. Fintech services like Wise come closest, typically charging 0.3–1% over the interbank/mid-market rate vs. banks' 2–5% markups.

Which is better for remittances: bank wire or services like Wise?

For most amounts, fintech remittance services (Wise, Remitly, WorldRemit) offer significantly better rates and lower flat fees than bank international wire transfers. Pakistani-specific services (ACE Money Transfer, Xpress Money) also compete well for PKR delivery. Compare the total amount received — not just the exchange rate — before choosing, as flat fees matter more for small transfers.

Does the PKR/USD rate include the interbank and open market difference?

This calculator uses internationally reported mid-market rates which typically reflect the interbank rate or a close approximation. Pakistan has historically had differences between its official/interbank rate and the open market rate — when exchange controls existed, the gap could be several rupees per dollar. The current rate shown reflects the internationally traded rate; local open market conditions may vary slightly.
