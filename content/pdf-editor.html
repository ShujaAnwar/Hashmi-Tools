Online PDF Editor — Annotate, Merge, Rotate, and Modify PDFs in Your Browser

PDF documents are the universal standard for sharing formatted documents — from government forms and bank statements to academic papers and contracts. Yet most people lack access to paid PDF editing software (Adobe Acrobat Pro costs $20+/month), and free alternatives are often either limited in features or require suspicious file uploads to unknown servers. A browser-based PDF editor running on open-source libraries (PDF.js + pdf-lib) solves both problems: full functionality, no cost, no upload required.

This editor processes your PDFs entirely in your browser. No PDF you open here is ever transmitted to a server. For sensitive documents — financial statements, legal contracts, identity documents, medical records — this is the only genuinely private editing option available without installing desktop software.

PDF Security: What Browser Editing Can and Can't Do

Browser-based PDF editing has clear capabilities and limitations worth understanding:

  • Can do: Add content layers (text, images, drawings) on top of existing PDF content; remove pages; rotate; merge multiple PDFs
  • Cannot do: Edit existing text that is already in the PDF (this requires access to the underlying PDF stream encoding); remove existing content (redaction requires specialised tools); fill interactive form fields in digitally signed PDFs without invalidating the signature
  • Password-protected PDFs: If a PDF has a user password (required to open), it must be unlocked before editing. Owner passwords (which restrict editing) may prevent saving changes in some libraries

Common PDF Editing Use Cases

  • Digital signatures — draw or paste your signature image onto contract PDFs for email return without printing, signing, and scanning
  • Form completion — add text fields to fill in government or bank forms that don't have fillable fields
  • Document clean-up — remove confidential pages before sharing a document or delete blank pages from scanned documents
  • Orientation correction — fix upside-down or sideways pages from mobile phone scans
  • Annotation — add notes, comments, or correction marks to academic papers, drafts, or review documents

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a digitally-drawn signature legally valid in Pakistan?

Under Pakistan's Electronic Transactions Ordinance 2002, electronic signatures have legal validity. However, the enforceability depends on context: many contracts and official documents still require wet (physical) signatures or NADRA-verified digital signatures for full legal effect. For commercial contracts, consult a lawyer about whether an electronically drawn signature is sufficient for your specific document type.

Why does the edited PDF appear larger in file size than the original?

When annotations are added using pdf-lib, the new content is embedded as an additional layer in the PDF structure. The original content is preserved intact, and new content is appended — so the file grows. For significant size reduction, run the PDF through the PDF compressor tool after editing.

Can I merge multiple PDFs with this tool?

Yes — the PDF Split & Merge tool (accessible from the PDF Tools hub) handles merging. Open multiple PDFs, arrange the page order, and download the combined document. The editor is focused on per-document annotation and page manipulation; the merge tool handles combining separate files.

Is my PDF document uploaded anywhere?

No. The editor uses PDF.js (Mozilla's open-source PDF renderer) for display and pdf-lib for modification — both run entirely in your browser. Your document data does not leave your device at any point. You can verify this by using browser DevTools → Network tab while editing: no upload requests will appear.

What is the maximum PDF size I can edit?

The tool handles PDFs up to approximately 50 MB efficiently in modern browsers. Very large PDFs (100+ pages at high resolution) may be slow to render on the first load but process fine once loaded. For extremely large documents (book-sized PDFs), consider splitting into sections first using the PDF Split tool.
