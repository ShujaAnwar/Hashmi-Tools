Complete Guide to Calculating Zakat on Savings & Investments in 2025

Hassan Hashmi May 2025 10 min read
Home › Blog › Zakat Guide 2025
Islamic Finance Zakat Pakistan 2025

Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam — an annual obligation on Muslims who hold wealth above a certain minimum threshold (the nisab) for one full Islamic lunar year. Despite being a fundamental religious duty, many Pakistani Muslims struggle to calculate Zakat correctly because modern assets like stocks, mutual funds, and cryptocurrency don't map neatly onto classical Islamic finance categories. This comprehensive guide covers every asset type you are likely to hold, explains the nisab for 2025, and walks you through the calculation step by step.

What Is Zakat and Who Must Pay It?

The word "Zakat" literally means "purification" in Arabic. It purifies your wealth by redistributing a portion to those in need. Unlike voluntary charity (sadaqah), Zakat is obligatory (fard) for every Muslim who meets three conditions:

  1. You are Muslim, adult, and of sound mind.
  2. You are free from debt that would reduce your net wealth below nisab.
  3. You have held wealth above the nisab threshold for one complete Hijri lunar year (hawl).

The standard Zakat rate is 2.5% of your total Zakatable net wealth at the end of the year. This simple percentage can make a huge difference in the lives of the poor, the needy, debtors, and travelers — the eight categories of Zakat recipients defined in the Quran (Surah At-Tawbah 9:60).

Understanding the Nisab in 2025

The nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a person must possess before Zakat becomes obligatory. There are two standards scholars use:

StandardWeightApprox. PKR Value (July 2025)
Gold Nisab85 grams of pure gold~ PKR 2,400,000
Silver Nisab595 grams of pure silver~ PKR 85,000

Which standard should you use? Most contemporary scholars recommend the silver nisab because it brings more Muslims into the Zakat system and benefits more recipients. However, the gold nisab is also a valid position followed by many scholars. Use whichever you follow based on your madhab or the guidance of your local Islamic scholar.

What Assets Are Zakatable?

Zakat applies to wealth that is "growing" or has the potential to grow. Broadly, the following categories are Zakatable:

1. Cash and Bank Savings

All cash in hand, current accounts, savings accounts, and money market funds is fully Zakatable at 2.5%. Include both PKR and any foreign currency holdings converted to PKR at the current rate. Note: If your bank deducts Zakat automatically (as required by Pakistani law for savings accounts), verify the amount deducted and top up if needed, since the bank uses the silver nisab and may calculate differently from your own reckoning.

2. Gold and Silver

Physical gold and silver — whether jewelry, coins, or bars — is Zakatable if it meets the nisab. There is a scholarly difference of opinion on gold jewelry worn regularly: the Hanafi school generally holds it is still Zakatable, while the Shafi'i and Maliki schools have more lenient positions. For safety, most contemporary scholars advise calculating Zakat on all gold jewelry.

Calculate based on the market value of your gold on the Zakat date, not the purchase price.

3. Stocks and Shares (Equities)

The most widely accepted method for stocks in Pakistan is the net assets / balance sheet method:

  • Look at the latest published financial statements of the company.
  • Identify the Zakatable assets (cash, receivables, inventory).
  • Calculate those as a percentage of total assets.
  • Multiply that percentage by the current market value of your shares.
  • Pay 2.5% on that result.

For shares in non-Shariah compliant companies, many scholars say you should either avoid such investments entirely, or if you hold them, pay Zakat on the full market value of the shares as a precaution.

4. Mutual Funds

For equity mutual funds (like those offered by Meezan, NBP Funds, or HBL Asset Management), apply the same balance sheet method. For money market funds or fixed-income funds, treat the unit value as cash and pay 2.5% on the full balance.

Islamic mutual funds typically publish Zakat deduction rates on their annual statements — use those figures if available.

5. Business Inventory and Receivables

If you run a business, include:

  • Inventory (goods for sale) at current market value or cost, whichever is lower
  • Receivables (money owed to you) — include only amounts you reasonably expect to collect within the next year
  • Cash on hand in the business

Exclude: fixed assets (machinery, vehicles, buildings) used in business operations — these are not Zakatable.

6. Rental Income and Property

The property itself (house, shop, plot) is not Zakatable. However, rental income received is Zakatable once received. Also, if you bought a property specifically to sell (investment property / plot), its current market value is Zakatable.

7. Provident Fund (PF) and Gratuity

Most Pakistani scholars hold that a government provident fund or employer-managed gratuity is Zakatable in the year you receive it (not while it accumulates, since you don't have control of it). Private voluntary PF contributions — where you control the funds — should be included each year.

When to Pay Zakat

Zakat is due once per Hijri lunar year. You should set a consistent date for yourself — many Muslims choose Ramadan for the spiritual blessing, while others pay on the anniversary of when they first became Zakatable. The key requirement is that wealth above nisab must have been held continuously for one full lunar year (approximately 354 days).

If your wealth dips below nisab during the year and then rises again, the one-year clock resets when it crosses the nisab again.

Common Mistakes in Zakat Calculation

  • Using the wrong nisab: If you're unsure, use the silver nisab — it's the more cautious approach.
  • Forgetting foreign currency: Convert and include any USD, EUR, or SAR savings.
  • Ignoring investment property: Many people forget that plots bought for investment are fully Zakatable.
  • Not deducting current liabilities: Short-term debts due within the year can be deducted from Zakatable wealth.
  • Paying on salary instead of wealth: Zakat is on accumulated savings/assets, not monthly income. Income is not directly Zakatable; what you save from it is.

Who Are the Eight Zakat Recipients?

The Quran (9:60) specifies eight categories: (1) the poor (fuqara), (2) the needy (masakeen), (3) those employed to collect Zakat, (4) those whose hearts are to be reconciled (new Muslims or allies), (5) freeing slaves, (6) those in debt, (7) in the way of Allah (fisabilillah — Islamic causes and education), and (8) stranded travelers. In Pakistan, reputable Zakat-distributing organizations include Edhi Foundation, Saylani Welfare Trust, Al-Khidmat Foundation, and the government's official Zakat Fund administered through AGPR.

Automatic Zakat Deduction in Pakistan

Under Pakistan's Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980, banks are required to automatically deduct Zakat from savings accounts at the start of Ramadan if the balance equals or exceeds the nisab-e-zakat set by the government. For 2025, the nisab-e-zakat was announced by the CZA (Central Zakat Administration). If you do not wish the bank to deduct (because you calculate and pay yourself), you must submit a CZ-50 declaration form affirming you follow a madhab that exempts savings account Zakat at source. This is entirely legal and allows you to pay Zakat yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Zakat obligatory on salary?

Zakat is not directly on salary/income — it is on accumulated, net wealth. However, the portion of your salary that you save and which remains above the nisab after one year is Zakatable.

Can I pay Zakat in installments?

Yes, you can spread payments across the year as long as the total equals your calculated Zakat. Many people divide the annual amount by 12 and pay monthly for convenience.

Is Zakat obligatory on a child's assets?

The Hanafi school says no; the Maliki and Shafi'i schools say a guardian must pay Zakat from a minor's Zakatable wealth. Consult your scholar.

What about crypto assets?

Most contemporary scholars treat cryptocurrency as a tradeable asset and apply Zakat on its current market value if held above nisab for a year. Given its volatility, use the value on your chosen Zakat date.

Conclusion

Calculating Zakat correctly in 2025 requires understanding which of your assets qualify, applying the right nisab (gold or silver), deducting liabilities, and paying 2.5% of the net.
