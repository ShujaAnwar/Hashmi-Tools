Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good typing speed?
Average typing speed is 40 WPM. A speed of 50-60 WPM is considered good for most office work. 70-80 WPM is professional grade, and anything above 90 WPM puts you in the top tier of typists. Speeds above 120 WPM are rare and expert-level.
How is WPM calculated?
WPM (Words Per Minute) is calculated by dividing the total number of correctly typed characters by 5 (the average word length) and then multiplying by 60 divided by the elapsed time in seconds. This standardizes the measurement regardless of actual word lengths.
What typing techniques improve speed?
The most effective technique is touch typing — using all 10 fingers without looking at the keyboard. Place your fingers on the home row (ASDF JKL;) and practice daily for 15-20 minutes. Focus on accuracy first, then gradually increase speed. Proper posture and ergonomics also help prevent fatigue.
What is typing accuracy and why does it matter?
Typing accuracy is the percentage of correctly typed characters compared to total characters typed. Accuracy matters more than raw speed in professional settings — fixing errors takes more time than typing slightly slower and accurately. Aim for 95%+ accuracy before focusing on speed.
What keyboard is best for fast typing?
Mechanical keyboards with tactile switches (Cherry MX Blue or Brown, Gateron, etc.) are popular among fast typists for their precise feedback. However, the best keyboard is one you're comfortable with. Many professional typists use standard membrane keyboards. A 60% or TKL layout can also help by reducing hand travel distance.

How the Typing Speed Test Works — Understanding WPM and Accuracy

The HashmiTools Typing Speed Test measures your typing performance across two key metrics: Words Per Minute (WPM) and Accuracy. WPM is calculated using the standard formula: (characters typed ÷ 5) ÷ elapsed minutes. The division by 5 converts raw character count into "standard words" (one standard word = 5 characters including spaces) — this normalisation makes WPM consistent across different text lengths and word complexity. Accuracy is the percentage of characters typed correctly out of total characters typed, calculated in real time as you type.

The test presents a randomly selected passage of approximately 150–200 words. You type in the input field below the passage; characters turn green as you type them correctly and red on errors. The timer starts automatically when you begin typing. At the end, your final WPM, accuracy percentage, error count, and elapsed time are displayed, along with a performance benchmark showing where your speed falls relative to typical typing population percentiles.

What Typing Speed Should You Aim For?

  • 20–30 WPM: Beginner level. Typical for hunt-and-peck typists or those just starting formal typing. Functional for casual use but limiting for professional productivity.
  • 40–55 WPM: Average. The typical speed for office workers who type daily but have not done formal touch-typing training. Adequate for most jobs.
  • 60–80 WPM: Above average. The target range for professionals whose work is heavily text-based — writers, journalists, data entry, customer service, programmers. Most touch-typists fall here.
  • 80–100 WPM: Fast. Competitive typists, experienced secretaries, and power users. At this speed, typing is no longer a bottleneck in any knowledge work.
  • 100+ WPM: Expert. Transcriptionists, stenographers, and competitive speed typists. The world record exceeds 200 WPM for short bursts.

In Pakistan's competitive job market, data entry and secretarial job listings frequently specify "minimum 40 WPM" as a requirement. For CSS exam typing (if applicable to your role), formal speed tests are administered. Regular daily practice with tools like this one measurably improves typing speed — research shows 20–30 minutes of deliberate practice daily yields significant improvement within 4–6 weeks.

How to Improve Your Typing Speed: Evidence-Based Techniques

  • Learn touch typing from the home row. The single biggest improvement comes from stopping looking at the keyboard. Touch typing — placing fingers on ASDF and JKL; as the home position and reaching all other keys from memory — is the foundation of fast typing. Free courses: TypingClub, Keybr, or 10FastFingers.
  • Prioritise accuracy over speed. Attempting to type faster than your accuracy allows increases errors, which actually reduces effective WPM after corrections. Type at the speed at which you make fewer than 2% errors, then gradually increase that speed.
  • Practice with common word lists. The top 200 most common English words account for roughly 50% of all text. Drilling these high-frequency words at high accuracy has outsized impact on practical typing speed.
  • Track your progress over time. The test saves your history locally. Review your scores weekly — consistent improvement (even 1–2 WPM per week) compounds significantly over months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my WPM seem lower than I expected?

This is the most common reaction from first-time testers. Most people significantly overestimate their typing speed when they haven't been formally tested before. Additionally, typing familiar text you're composing yourself feels faster than copying unfamiliar text from a passage (which is what a typing test measures). The test passage also includes uncommon words and punctuation that slow most typists down relative to their everyday typing. Your test score is a baseline — not an absolute ceiling. With focused practice, most people improve 10–20 WPM within a few weeks of daily testing.

Does this test work on mobile phones?

The typing test is accessible on mobile browsers, but mobile virtual keyboards produce significantly lower WPM scores than physical keyboards for most users. If your goal is to measure and improve professional typing speed, always test on a physical keyboard. Mobile touch-typing is a different skill with its own speed ceiling — most people top out at 40–50 WPM on mobile keyboards regardless of experience, while the same users can achieve 60–80 WPM on a physical keyboard. For mobile typing speed practice specifically, dedicated mobile keyboard training apps (SwiftKey training mode, GBoard speed challenges) are more appropriate.

How is accuracy calculated and why does it matter as much as speed?

Accuracy is calculated as (correct characters ÷ total characters typed) × 100. It matters as much as raw speed because errors have a compounding cost: every error requires you to backspace, retype, and lose 1–3 seconds depending on how far back the error is noticed. At 80 WPM with 95% accuracy, your effective output is similar to 65 WPM at 99% accuracy in terms of clean text produced per minute. Professional typing assessments (for jobs, competitions) almost always score on net WPM — speed minus penalties for errors — rather than gross WPM. Always aim for 97%+ accuracy before trying to increase speed.

What keyboard layout should I use — QWERTY, Dvorak, or Colemak?

For most users, QWERTY is the correct answer — not because it's the most efficient layout, but because QWERTY is universal. Every physical and virtual keyboard, every shared computer, and every job environment uses QWERTY by default. The productivity benefit of switching to Dvorak or Colemak (estimated 5–10% speed improvement after a 3–6 month relearning period) is rarely worth the cost of being unable to type effectively on any keyboard that isn't yours. The exception: if you are building typing skills from scratch and have no QWERTY muscle memory yet, Colemak is worth considering for its ergonomic advantages. For anyone with existing QWERTY skills above 50 WPM, improving accuracy and technique within QWERTY will yield better returns than switching layouts.

Can I use this typing test for a job application requirement?

HashmiTools Typing Test provides a genuine measurement of your typing speed and accuracy under realistic test conditions, and your results are displayed with a timestamp. However, for formal employment verification (where an employer requires certified proof of typing speed), most organisations administer their own internal typing test or use a formal testing centre. This tool is best used for personal practice, self-assessment, and preparation for formal tests — not as a substitute for an employer-administered formal typing certification. That said, consistently scoring your target WPM here is strong evidence that you will meet the requirement in a formal assessment.

How long should I practise each day to improve my typing speed?

Research in skill acquisition suggests that 20–30 minutes of focused deliberate practice daily is more effective than occasional long sessions. "Deliberate practice" means working slightly above your current comfortable speed — not just cruising at your existing speed, but actively pushing into the zone where you're making some errors and correcting your technique. Daily consistency over 6–8 weeks produces the most dramatic improvement. After reaching your target speed (typically 60–70 WPM for professional purposes), 10 minutes of maintenance practice a few times per week is sufficient to preserve the skill.
